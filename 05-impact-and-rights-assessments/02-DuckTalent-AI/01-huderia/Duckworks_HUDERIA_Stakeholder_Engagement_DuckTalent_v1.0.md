## 3. Positionality Reflection

| **Current limitation.** The present assessment team is dominated by Duckworks internal business, technology, risk, legal, privacy and security perspectives. It does not represent the lived experience of external applicants, persons with disabilities, linguistically diverse candidates, or other potentially marginalized groups. This is a material evidence gap, not a reason to infer their views. |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| **Reflection question**                       | **Duckworks response / action**                                                                                                                                                          |
|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Where does the team hold institutional power? | Duckworks controls job criteria, system design, vendor selection, application workflow and employment decisions; applicants have substantially less influence.                           |
| What perspectives are missing?                | External applicants, unsuccessful candidates, accessibility/disability perspectives, linguistic/credential diversity, and potentially other protected/vulnerable groups.                 |
| How could this shape the assessment?          | Internal teams may understate friction, opacity, stigma, procedural burden or the practical difficulty of challenging an AI-supported decision.                                          |
| Corrective action                             | Engage representative stakeholders before real pilot; use accessible methods; document disagreement and unresolved concerns; do not treat internal SMEs as proxies for affected persons. |

## 4. Engagement Objectives

1.  Validate whether the identified adverse impacts reflect real applicant concerns and identify omitted harms.

2.  Test whether proposed notices and explanations are understandable, useful and appropriately timed.

3.  Understand barriers to correction, challenge, accommodation and human reconsideration.

4.  Assess whether job criteria, ranking logic and recruitment workflow create disproportionate burdens or exclusion.

5.  Evaluate whether proposed mitigation measures are practical and whether they introduce new harms.

6.  Identify acceptable monitoring and feedback mechanisms without unnecessary collection of sensitive data.

## 7. Engagement Quality Criteria

- Meaningful involvement: affected-person input can change design, controls or the deployment decision.

- Equality and non-discrimination: participation methods accommodate protected characteristics and vulnerability factors.

- Empowerment: information is understandable and accessible; participants can raise concerns safely.

- Transparency: objectives, scope, limitations and how input will be used are explained.

- Accountability: every material action arising from engagement has an owner and closure evidence.
