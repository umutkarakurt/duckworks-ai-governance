# Duckworks AIMS Internal Audit Procedure

Document ID: AIMS-IA-PRO-001  
Version: 1.0  
Effective date: 2026-09-09  
Process owner: Head of Internal Audit (role to be formally assigned)  
Approval authority: Chief Risk & Compliance Officer  
Review cycle: Annual and after material AIMS change

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how Duckworks plans, performs, reports and follows up internal audits of its AI management system. Audits assess whether selected arrangements are defined, implemented and maintained, and whether objective evidence supports management claims. They do not themselves authorize deployment, accept risk or establish certification.

## 2. Scope

The procedure applies to the AIMS, the seven portfolio entries, 21 risk scenarios, 45 canonical controls, linked evidence, governance decisions and related corrective actions. Supplier, privacy, security, product, workforce and legal topics are included when they affect AIMS objectives or risks.

## 3. Principles

1. Risk-based planning: priority reflects risk, status claims, evidence maturity, change, previous findings and regulatory exposure.
2. Independence: auditors do not audit work they own, operate, prepared or approved.
3. Competence: assigned auditors must demonstrate relevant audit, AI-governance and subject-matter competence.
4. Evidence-based conclusions: source labels and management statements are not substitutes for retrievable evidence.
5. Traceability: every test links criteria, procedure, population, sample, result, exception, conclusion and reviewer.
6. Human authority: automation may validate structure and consistency but cannot approve conclusions or close findings.
7. Proportionality: test depth and frequency reflect risk while preserving the full audit universe.

## 4. Roles

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Chief Risk & Compliance Officer | Approves the programme, resolves escalations and safeguards access |
| Head of Internal Audit / independent designee | Owns the audit universe and programme; assigns competent independent auditors; approves reports |
| Audit lead | Plans and performs the engagement; maintains workpapers; communicates findings |
| Control owner | Provides complete evidence, management response and remediation; does not close own finding |
| AI Governance Lead | Maintains crosswalk and evidence index after approved outcomes |
| Finding validator | Independently tests closure evidence and recommends closure or reopening |

## 5. Annual planning

At least annually, and after a material trigger, the audit owner shall:

1. reconcile the audit universe to the approved control-applicability register;
2. score priority using risk exposure, implementation assertion, evidence maturity, change and prior findings;
3. assign an engagement, planned date, frequency, objective, scope and resource need to every control;
4. identify independence or competence gaps and mitigation;
5. submit the programme for human approval; and
6. preserve the approved version and supersession record.

Priority rules for v1.7 are:

- P1 Immediate: unsupported `Implemented` status or similarly urgent assurance conflict;
- P2 High: linked synthetic evidence, or partial/ad hoc implementation without linked evidence;
- P3 Medium: planned or not-implemented controls requiring readiness/design review.

## 6. Engagement planning

Each engagement plan shall state the objective, scope, exclusions, criteria, population, sampling basis, methods, team, independence declaration, competence needs, timetable, deliverables, communication route and decision boundary. Criteria must be accessible and authorized. Local AIMS themes must not be represented as ISO clause numbers.

## 7. Fieldwork and evidence

Evidence must be sufficient, reliable, relevant and useful. The auditor shall record:

- the complete population and period, including how completeness was tested;
- the sampling method and rationale;
- the exact item, version or evidence ID inspected;
- who performed and reviewed the original control;
- exceptions, overrides, escalations and zero-event assertions;
- recalculation or reperformance where appropriate; and
- limitations, contradictory evidence and unresolved requests.

Absence of evidence is not automatically proof that a control failed. It prevents substantiation of the asserted state and requires an evidence request, status correction or finding as appropriate.

## 8. Findings and ratings

Every finding records condition, criteria, cause or cause gap, effect, severity, owner, due date, response, action, closure evidence and status.

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical | Immediate material exposure or prohibited operation requiring executive escalation |
| High | Material control/evidence gap that could support an unsafe or overstated assurance decision |
| Moderate | Significant weakness requiring tracked remediation but not immediate stop-use |
| Low | Limited weakness or improvement opportunity |

Management owns remediation. Disagreement is recorded, not overwritten. Overdue High or Critical findings are escalated to the CRCO and the relevant governance forum.

## 9. Reporting and follow-up

The audit report shall distinguish design, implementation, operating effectiveness and outcome effectiveness. It shall state scope limitations and prohibit generalized conclusions beyond the tested population and period. Findings remain open until an independent validator inspects the required closure evidence. Automated checks may flag completeness but record zero automatic closures.

## 10. Records and retention

Plans, requests, workpapers, evidence references, reports, responses, actions, validation records and approvals are controlled AIMS records. Stable IDs, dates, owners and supersession links are required. Access and retention follow applicable legal, security and records requirements; this synthetic package does not set production retention periods.

## 11. Triggers

Re-plan or initiate an audit after material system, supplier, model, data, legal, organizational, risk, control, evidence or incident change; a failed validation; an overdue High/Critical finding; or a management request. Changes to the audit programme require documented approval.
