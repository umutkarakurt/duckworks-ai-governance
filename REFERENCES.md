## 1. Core AI Governance & Risk Management Frameworks

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

- **Publisher:** U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
- **Type:** Voluntary AI risk management framework
- **Primary use in Duckworks:** Overall AI risk-governance structure; Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage functions.
- **Official framework page:** https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- **Official publication:** https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10

- **NIST AI Resource Center:** https://airc.nist.gov/

- **Type:** Implementation guidance supporting the AI RMF
- **Primary use in Duckworks:** Candidate governance actions, evidence expectations, and implementation practices.
- **Official link:** https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/

### NIST AI 600-1 — Generative AI Profile

- **Publisher:** NIST

## 3. European Union — Core AI & Data Legislation

### EU Artificial Intelligence Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

- **Type:** Binding EU regulation
- **Primary use in Duckworks:** Legal classification, prohibited practices, high-risk AI requirements, transparency, deployer/provider obligations, AI literacy, governance, and post-market responsibilities.
- **Current consolidated text (27 July 2026):** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/eng
- **Original Official Journal text:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng
- **EUR-Lex summary:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/rules-for-trustworthy-artificial-intelligence-in-the-eu.html

**Duckworks relevance:**  
Particularly important to **DuckTalent AI**, because systems intended for recruitment, candidate selection, filtering, or evaluation require specific legal classification analysis. It is also relevant to QuackBot, PondGPT, DuckDesign AI, WingInspect Vision, and other AI systems depending on intended purpose and Duckworks' legal role.

## 10. Recommended Evidence Hierarchy

When Duckworks documents a regulatory or framework conclusion, use sources in the following order:

1. **Binding legal text** — EUR-Lex / Official Journal.
2. **Official regulator or competent-authority guidance** — e.g., EDPB, ENISA.
3. **Formal standards and frameworks** — e.g., ISO/IEC, NIST.
4. **Recognized technical threat/control references** — e.g., MITRE ATLAS, OWASP.
5. **Secondary commentary** — only for additional interpretation; do not use as the primary basis for a legal or compliance conclusion.

For legislation that has been amended, prefer the **current consolidated text for working analysis**, while retaining the original Official Journal act and relevant amending acts in the legal evidence trail.

## 11. Maintenance Rules for This File

- Review links and legal versions **at least quarterly** during the portfolio project.
- Re-check the EU AI Act before making any statement about application dates, classifications, or obligations.
- Record the date of legal research in each AI impact/risk assessment.
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - **binding legal requirements**;
  - **regulatory guidance**;
  - **voluntary standards/frameworks**;
  - **internal Duckworks policy requirements**.
- Do not state that ISO/NIST/OWASP alignment establishes legal compliance.
- Do not treat Duckworks' internal **High/Critical risk rating** as equivalent to the EU AI Act's legal concept of a **high-risk AI system**.
- For EU directives such as NIS 2, employment-equality directives, and the Product Liability Directive, assess the applicable **national implementing law** before making a real-world compliance determination.
