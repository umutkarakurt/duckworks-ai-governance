## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how Duckworks receives, protects, assesses, investigates, escalates and records reports of suspected AI-related wrongdoing or serious governance failures. Its purpose is to make it safe to raise concerns before AI-related harm is concealed, normalized or allowed to continue.

The policy supports Project W.I.N.G.’s objective that material AI risks, incidents, control failures, complaints and unregistered use can be escalated and evidenced. It complements, rather than replaces, Duckworks incident response, HR grievance, privacy, security, product-safety, procurement and AI governance processes.

### 2.1 What this policy does not claim

- It does not claim that every AI complaint is legally protected whistleblowing.

- It does not determine the competent national authority for every Duckworks establishment.

- It does not replace national implementing law, collective agreements, worker-representation rights or sector-specific reporting rules.

- It does not restrict a person from reporting directly to an external competent authority where applicable.

- It does not make Internal Audit the owner of reporting channels or investigations.

## 3. Who May Report

Duckworks permits reports from employees and, where relevant to a work-related context, former workers, applicants, contractors, subcontractors, suppliers, self-employed persons, trainees, volunteers, shareholders, members of governing bodies and other persons permitted by applicable law. Duckworks may accept a wider category of AI concerns as an organizational practice even where statutory whistleblower protection is not established.

### 4.1 Reports, complaints and grievances

Not every concern is a whistleblowing report. Routine service complaints, ordinary technical defects, interpersonal grievances or disagreements over legitimate management decisions may be routed to another process. However, a report must not be downgraded merely because it also contains an HR grievance, customer complaint, privacy request or incident. Mixed cases should be triaged so that any protected whistleblowing element remains protected.

### 5.1 Anonymous reporting

Duckworks will accept and assess anonymous AI whistleblowing reports as an internal organizational practice where technically and legally feasible. The EU Directive leaves Member States discretion on whether entities must accept and follow up anonymous reports, subject to other Union-law obligations. Legal must therefore validate local rules. An anonymous reporter who is later identified may still qualify for statutory protection where the applicable conditions are met.

### 5.2 External reporting

Duckworks will provide clear, accessible information on external reporting to the competent authority or authorities applicable to the relevant country and subject matter. A reporter is not required by this policy to report internally first when applicable law permits direct external reporting. Duckworks Legal shall maintain the country-specific authority information outside this policy so it can be updated without rewriting the policy.

### 5.3 Public disclosure

Public disclosure can carry different legal conditions and risks. This policy does not advise a person to make or avoid a public disclosure. Where needed, Duckworks will make information available on external channels and legal protections, but the conditions for protected public disclosure must be determined under applicable law.

## 7. Confidentiality and Need-to-Know Handling

- Reporter identity and any information from which identity can be directly or indirectly inferred will be restricted to authorized personnel handling the report.

- The AI Governance Committee should normally receive the allegation, risk and action status without the reporter’s identity unless identity is genuinely necessary for lawful follow-up.

- System owners or managers implicated by a report must not receive information that unnecessarily identifies the reporter.

- If disclosure of identity is legally necessary and proportionate, the applicable legal safeguards and notification requirements must be followed.

- Facilitators and other protected persons should receive comparable confidentiality treatment where applicable.

- The identity and rights of the person who is the subject of an allegation must also be protected consistent with applicable law and a fair investigation.

### 7.1 Conflicts of interest

## 8. Prohibition of Retaliation

Duckworks prohibits retaliation, attempted retaliation and threats of retaliation against a person because they reported, assisted with, or participated in the handling of an AI-related concern. This policy protection applies to reports within Duckworks’ policy scope; statutory protection is determined by applicable law.

- Dismissal, suspension, demotion or withholding promotion.

- Unjustified changes in duties, location, pay, working hours or access to training.

- Unfair performance reviews, disciplinary action or adverse references.

- Coercion, intimidation, harassment, ostracism, discrimination or unfair treatment.

- Blacklisting, reputational harm or interference with future work opportunities.

- Unjustified termination or non-renewal of a contract or supplier relationship.

- Threats or pressure to withdraw, alter or suppress a report.

- Misuse of AI monitoring, HR analytics or access logs to identify or target a reporter.

### 8.1 Retaliation safeguards

☐ Reporter-facing case communications will include a clear route for reporting suspected retaliation.

☐ HR and Legal will assess material employment actions involving an identified reporter where a retaliation risk has been flagged, without creating unnecessary disclosure of the report.

☐ AI or analytics tools must not be used to infer or identify a confidential reporter unless specifically authorized for a lawful investigation purpose.

☐ Confirmed retaliation is treated as a serious policy violation and escalated for disciplinary or contractual action subject to applicable law.

## 9. Case Intake, Triage and Investigation

| **Step**                | **Required action**                                                                                                               |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Receive             | Securely register the report and preserve confidentiality.                                                                        |
| 2\. Acknowledge         | Confirm receipt where contact is available.                                                                                       |
| 3\. Protect             | Assess immediate retaliation, safety, privacy, security and evidence-preservation risks.                                          |
| 4\. Classify            | Determine whether the matter may fall within statutory whistleblowing scope, Duckworks AI policy scope, both, or another process. |
| 5\. Conflict check      | Ensure the handler/investigator is independent of persons implicated.                                                             |
| 6\. AI severity triage  | Identify affected AI system, current gate, possible harm, legal/safety/security/privacy/HR implications and need for stop-use.    |
| 7\. Investigate / refer | Assign an impartial investigator or specialist team; referrals are need-to-know and identity-minimized.                           |
| 8\. Remediate           | Contain harmful use, correct controls, update assessments, preserve evidence, initiate incident/change management where needed.   |
| 9\. Feedback            | Provide appropriate status/outcome feedback within applicable timelines.                                                          |
| 10\. Close & monitor    | Document rationale, actions, residual risk, retaliation monitoring, lessons learned and governance updates.                       |

### 9.1 AI-specific triage questions

☐ Which AI ID, vendor/model, business process and owner are implicated?

☐ Is the AI registered and operating within its approved purpose and lifecycle gate?

☐ Could continued use cause physical harm, discrimination, rights impact, personal/confidential data exposure or significant customer harm?

☐ Does the report indicate a prohibited/unlawful practice, potential AI Act infringement or another legal breach?

☐ Has a control, test result, risk rating, model limitation, incident or approval record been concealed or manipulated?

☐ Is there evidence of prompt injection, data leakage, model compromise, unsafe autonomy or loss of human oversight?

☐ Does the concern require immediate suspension, human fallback, vendor containment or preservation of technical logs?

## 12. Fair Treatment of All Parties

A report is an allegation, not a finding. Persons named in a report are entitled to fair treatment, appropriate confidentiality, and the applicable rights of defence and response. Investigations should be evidence-based, proportionate and free from conflicts of interest.

A reporter is not expected to prove a case before reporting. Honest mistakes or reports that are not substantiated will not, by themselves, result in adverse action. Knowingly reporting false information may lead to disciplinary or contractual consequences subject to applicable law. This must not be used to deter reasonable reporting or punish an unproven allegation.

## 13. Personal Data, Records and Evidence

- Collect only personal data reasonably necessary to receive, assess, investigate and follow up the report.

- Manifestly irrelevant personal data should not be collected; if collected accidentally, it should be deleted without undue delay where required.

- Whistleblowing case records must be segregated from ordinary AI project repositories where broad access would undermine confidentiality.

- Retention must be necessary and proportionate and comply with applicable national law, GDPR and litigation/investigation holds.

- Oral reports or meetings should be documented in accordance with applicable legal requirements, consent rules and reporter rights to review/agree records where relevant.

- Technical evidence should preserve provenance, timestamps, system/version identifiers, chain of custody and integrity without exposing reporter identity unnecessarily.

## 15. Training, Awareness and Accessibility

- All workers receive clear information on protected reporting channels, confidentiality and prohibition of retaliation.

- Managers receive specific training that concerns must be escalated and must not be suppressed, investigated informally outside the process, or used against the reporter.

- AI system owners, developers, data teams, recruiters, engineers, inspectors and customer-support staff receive examples relevant to their AI use cases.

- Reporting information must be accessible to contractors and other eligible work-related persons where applicable.

- Accessibility, language and reasonable-accommodation needs should be considered so that the reporting mechanism is usable in practice.

## 17. Policy Breaches

Failure to preserve confidentiality, retaliation, deliberate suppression of a report, evidence destruction, obstruction of follow-up, unauthorized investigation, or misuse of case information may result in disciplinary or contractual action subject to applicable law. Material breaches may also trigger AI incident response, security investigation, privacy response, legal escalation or external reporting obligations.

## 18. Review and Maintenance

The CRCO and General Counsel will review this policy at least annually and after a material change in applicable whistleblower law, the EU AI Act, Duckworks reporting channels, organizational structure, or a significant case that identifies a design weakness. Country-specific competent-authority information should be maintained separately and reviewed more frequently as needed.
