## 1. Purpose and Authority

This policy establishes mandatory enterprise requirements for governing AI at Duckworks. It defines what must be true before AI is acquired, developed, piloted, deployed, materially changed, operated, suspended or retired. It is designed to enable controlled innovation while ensuring that legal, security, privacy, fairness, safety, operational, third-party, ethical and evidence risks receive proportionate governance.

This policy sits below the Duckworks Enterprise AI Governance Charter and above operational procedures and technical standards. It does not replace the Charter, the AI Responsible Use Policy, enterprise risk management, the ISMS, procurement, privacy governance, product-safety processes, HR governance, incident response, records management or Internal Audit.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to Duckworks personnel and third parties acting on behalf of Duckworks when AI is proposed, acquired, built, configured, tested, used, changed or retired.

- Internally developed machine-learning and AI systems.

- Third-party AI services, hosted models, APIs and AI-enabled SaaS functionality.

- Generative AI, predictive machine learning, computer vision, coding assistants, embedded AI and agentic or automated workflows.

- AI embedded in engineering, manufacturing, customer service, supply chain, HR, software development, productivity or other business processes.

- Proofs of concept, experiments, pilots, production systems and material changes to existing AI.

- Public or consumer AI used for Duckworks work, data, code, credentials, devices, customer-facing content or decisions.

- Discovered or unregistered shadow AI.

### 2.1 Material AI use

For Duckworks internal governance, an AI use is material when it can materially affect people, product or physical safety, confidential or personal data, significant financial or operational decisions, customer outcomes, legal obligations, product quality, or a meaningful third-party/security dependency. This is an internal governance definition and is not a statutory classification.

## 4. Key Definitions

| **Term**                   | **Duckworks meaning**                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| AI system                  | For this policy, software or a service using AI/ML techniques to generate, predict, classify, rank, recommend, detect, summarize, automate or support decisions. Internal scope definition only.                                  |
| Approved AI                | AI that has completed the required Duckworks intake and review and is authorized for a stated purpose, lifecycle stage, data boundary, users and operating conditions.                                                            |
| Business / AI System Owner | First-line role accountable for intended purpose, business outcome, affected stakeholders, operating controls, treatment actions and use within the approved boundary.                                                            |
| Technical Owner            | Role accountable for architecture, model/service implementation, technical validation, technical controls, version/change evidence and monitoring.                                                                                |

| Material change            | A change to intended purpose, model/provider, data, affected population, permissions, integrations, autonomy, deployment geography, decision authority or other factor capable of altering risk, controls or legal applicability. |
| Current residual risk      | Risk remaining after controls that are actually implemented and supported by evidence.                                                                                                                                            |
| Target residual risk       | Expected risk after planned treatment; it is not current-state risk.                                                                                                                                                              |
| Shadow AI                  | AI use occurring outside approved intake, procurement, security, privacy or governance processes.                                                                                                                                 |

## 5. AI Governance Principles

| **Principle**                   | **Mandatory policy rule**                                                                                                                               |
|---------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Controlled innovation           | Governance shall enable useful experimentation while preventing unmanaged material exposure.                                                            |
| Intended purpose first          | Governance shall evaluate the actual use, decision role, autonomy, affected people, data and operating context rather than relying on model type alone. |
| Proportionality                 | Governance depth shall increase with impact, uncertainty, criticality, autonomy, affected-person sensitivity and legal significance.                    |
| Human accountability            | Consequential decisions remain assigned to accountable human roles unless a separately approved and lawful design changes that boundary.                |
| No averaging away severe harm   | A severe safety, rights, privacy, security or product scenario shall not be diluted by averaging it with lower-impact scenarios.                        |
| Evidence before reliance        | Control effectiveness, validation and approval claims must be supported by evidence. Planned controls receive no current-state credit.                  |
| Lifecycle governance            | Approval is not permanent. Material change, incident, drift, vendor change, data change, control failure or legal change can trigger reassessment.      |
| Independent challenge           | First-line ownership, second-line challenge and third-line assurance shall remain distinct.                                                             |
| Data responsibility             | AI data shall be authorized, minimized, classified, traceable and protected according to purpose and sensitivity.                                       |
| Security and resilience         | AI-specific threats and conventional security threats shall be managed through Duckworks' security governance and ISMS.                                 |
| Transparency and contestability | People shall receive appropriate transparency, human access and challenge/remedy mechanisms when required by law, policy or consequence of use.         |
| Responsible supply chain        | Third-party AI dependency does not transfer Duckworks accountability for its own use of the system.                                                     |

### 6.1 Segregation rules

- Every material governance activity shall have one clearly accountable role.

- A Business or Technical Owner may propose controls and risk acceptance but shall not unilaterally self-approve High or Critical current residual risk.

- Legal classification shall not be determined by the AI Governance Committee through risk scoring.

- Internal Audit shall not be assigned control ownership, operating approval or first/second-line remediation responsibility.

## 10. Personal Data and Privacy

- Where AI processes personal data, Duckworks shall apply GDPR and other applicable privacy requirements independently of the AI Act.

- Purpose, lawful basis, transparency, minimization, data quality, retention, access, processor roles, transfers and security shall be addressed through the established privacy governance process.

- A GDPR DPIA shall be performed when the Article 35 threshold is met. This policy does not substitute for a DPIA.

- Personal, applicant, employee or customer data shall not be submitted to unapproved public AI services.

- Vendor use of Duckworks data for shared-model training or provider improvement is prohibited by default unless explicitly approved under the applicable contract/DPA and governance process.

## 11. Data and Model Governance

- Material AI datasets, knowledge corpora and reference sources shall have documented purpose, owner, provenance, classification and quality expectations.

- Where Duckworks trains or fine-tunes models, training/validation/test data roles and dataset versions shall be traceable and evaluation leakage shall be controlled.

- RAG and knowledge sources shall preserve source authorization, freshness, provenance and deletion/change propagation where material.

- Material changes to training/reference data, features, labels, corpora, embeddings or input categories shall trigger change screening.

- AI Bills of Materials or equivalent dependency records shall be maintained where proportional to system complexity, supplier dependency and assurance need.

## 12. AI Security and Resilience

AI security shall be integrated into Duckworks' information-security management and secure engineering practices rather than operated as an isolated parallel control system.

| **Security area**     | **Policy expectation**                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Access and identity   | Least privilege, strong authentication, authorization boundaries, privileged administration and periodic review.                                                                                                 |
| Data protection       | DLP, encryption, secrets protection, environment separation and controlled egress appropriate to sensitivity.                                                                                                    |
| AI-specific attacks   | Threat modelling and testing for prompt injection, indirect prompt injection, retrieval poisoning, data/model poisoning, adversarial manipulation, unsafe generated code and model/service abuse where relevant. |
| Integrations / agents | Tools, APIs, plugins and agentic actions require explicit permissions, bounded authority, logging and safe failure paths.                                                                                        |
| Supply chain          | Models, libraries, APIs, services, plugins, data and vendor dependencies are inventoried and assessed proportionately.                                                                                           |
| Monitoring / response | Security events and AI incidents must be detectable, triaged, contained and linked to system/component/version evidence.                                                                                         |
| Resilience            | Material systems require fallback, suspension or rollback appropriate to consequence and dependency.                                                                                                             |

## 13. Human Oversight and Consequential Decisions

| **Oversight element** | **Mandatory expectation**                                                                                                                   |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Competence            | Human reviewers must understand the task, AI role, limitations, relevant risks and escalation route.                                        |
| Information           | Reviewers must receive sufficient context to evaluate the output rather than merely approve a score or recommendation.                      |
| Authority             | Reviewers must be able to disregard, override, reverse, stop or escalate the AI-supported action where the design requires human authority. |
| Time and workload     | Governance shall consider whether volume, time pressure, interface design or incentives make human review merely ceremonial.                |
| Automation bias       | Higher-impact uses shall include controls/training to reduce inappropriate reliance on AI output.                                           |
| Accountability        | The accountable human or organizational role remains identifiable in the resulting business decision.                                       |
| Record                | Consequential decisions shall retain the review/override evidence required by the relevant domain process.                                  |

## 14. Third-Party AI Governance

- AI procurement, renewal and material vendor change shall pass the Duckworks third-party AI due-diligence process before continued material reliance.

- Due diligence shall address security, privacy, data use, model training/improvement, retention, subprocessors, hosting/locations, model/service changes, intellectual property, resilience, incident notification, assurance evidence and exit.

- Contracts and DPAs shall include the applicable Duckworks AI requirements and legally mandatory terms where triggered.

- Supplier certifications or attestations are evidence inputs, not substitutes for Duckworks use-case risk assessment.

- Material provider/model/data-use/subprocessor/hosting changes shall trigger the AI Change Management Process.

- Exit planning shall include data return/deletion, evidence preservation, alternative service/manual fallback and removal of access.

## 15. Transparency, AI Literacy and Affected-Party Communication

### 15.1 AI literacy

Duckworks shall provide role-based AI literacy appropriate to the person's technical knowledge, responsibilities, context of use and the people who may be affected. Training evidence shall be retained for material roles. Training depth shall be greater for system owners, developers, reviewers, recruiters, engineers, inspectors, customer-facing users and incident responders.

### 15.2 Transparency and contestability

- Users and affected persons shall receive legally required AI disclosures and any additional transparency needed for informed and safe reliance.

- Customer-facing AI shall disclose or otherwise make AI interaction apparent where required by law/policy and shall provide human escalation for consequential or contested matters.

- People materially affected by AI-supported decisions shall have a route to ask questions, raise complaints or seek human reconsideration where required by law or Duckworks governance.

- Generated or manipulated content shall be marked/disclosed where applicable legal requirements or approved communication standards require it.

### 17.1 Mandatory reassessment triggers

- Change in intended purpose, autonomy or formal decision authority.

- New model, provider, version, fine-tune or material configuration.

- New training/reference data, features, labels, RAG corpus, connector, API, tool or permission.

- Expansion to new users, affected populations, geography, language, product line or consequential process.

- Material incident, near miss, complaint, discriminatory outcome, safety event, confidential-data leak or loss of human oversight.

- Drift, error change, control failure, evidence expiry or invalidated project assumption.

- Material vendor/subprocessor/hosting/data-use/retention change.

- Change in applicable law, regulator guidance or Duckworks risk appetite.

## 18. Change Management, Incidents, Suspension and Retirement

- Material AI changes shall follow the approved AI Change Management Process before permanent implementation.

- Emergency changes may use the approved emergency path but must retain minimum authorization, logging, verification and retrospective review.

- AI incidents shall use the approved AI Runbooks and enterprise security/privacy/safety/HR incident processes as applicable.

- Stop-use authority shall be clear for systems capable of material harm.

- Suspension shall be considered where current residual risk is outside tolerance, required controls fail, material assumptions are invalid, legal use becomes uncertain, or monitoring is insufficient to provide reasonable oversight.

- Retirement shall include access removal, vendor exit, data/record disposition, evidence preservation and inventory closure.

## 19. Experimentation and Shadow AI

Duckworks supports controlled experimentation. Experimentation is not exempt from information classification, security, privacy, procurement, intellectual-property or legal requirements.

- Low-impact experiments may use a simplified intake route where approved by the governance framework.

- Experiments shall not create a de facto production dependency without lifecycle approval.

- Restricted/confidential/personal data shall not be entered into unapproved public AI.

- Shadow AI must be discovered and decomposed into individual material use cases; AI-007 is not treated as one homogeneous system.

- Where shadow AI creates an active confidentiality, privacy, safety or security exposure, containment takes priority over normal intake.

- Duckworks should provide practical approved alternatives so governance controls do not simply drive use into less visible channels.

### 20.1 Evidence quality rules

- Evidence shall identify system/AI ID, relevant version, owner, scope, status and source.

- Evidence that is missing, stale, unverified or based on assumption shall be labelled rather than treated as implemented proof.

- Target-state controls shall remain distinguishable from currently operating controls.

- Evidence retention shall follow legal, records, privacy, security, product/safety and contractual requirements; this policy does not impose one universal retention period.

## 21. Exceptions, Risk Acceptance and Non-Compliance

### 21.1 Policy exceptions

- Exceptions must identify the policy requirement, reason, scope, risk, affected systems, compensating controls, owner, approver, expiry and monitoring.

- Exceptions shall be time-bounded and reviewed before expiry.

- An exception cannot authorize a breach of applicable law.

- Material exceptions involving High/Critical risk or consequential rights/safety impact require AI Governance Committee / executive escalation according to delegated authority.

### 21.2 Risk acceptance

Risk acceptance follows the approved Duckworks risk methodology. Critical current residual risk is normally outside production tolerance; exceptional acceptance, where permitted at all, requires executive authority and separate legal/specialist review. Target residual risk shall not be presented as current residual risk.

### 21.3 Policy violations

Material or repeated violation may result in suspension of the AI system or access, remediation requirements, incident investigation, procurement/vendor action, disciplinary or contractual action subject to applicable law, and escalation to executive governance. Good-faith reporting of AI concerns must not be treated as misconduct and is protected under the Duckworks AI Whistleblower Policy and applicable law.

### 22.1 Board / executive reporting principle

Executive reporting shall support prioritization and decision-making rather than present technical metrics without context. Material uncertainty, blocked systems, overdue treatment, systemic vendor exposure, repeated incidents and evidence gaps shall be visible.

## 23. Independent Assurance

- Internal Audit may assess whether the AI governance framework is appropriately designed, implemented, operating consistently and supported by evidence.

- Internal Audit shall not own the inventory, risk methodology, approval gates, remediation actions or operational controls it may later audit.

- Second-line monitoring and challenge shall not be described as independent third-line assurance.

- External assurance or certification may be used where useful but shall not replace Duckworks accountability for system-specific governance.
