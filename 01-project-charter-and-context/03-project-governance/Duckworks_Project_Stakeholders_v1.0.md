## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the stakeholders required to design, approve, operate, challenge, monitor, and independently assure the Duckworks AI Governance Framework. It also identifies stakeholder groups that may be affected by AI-enabled decisions and therefore require representation in impact and governance activities.

Duckworks principle: “Every material AI use case should have a named owner, an informed challenger, and a clearly identified population that may be affected.”

## 2. Stakeholder Governance Model

- **Executive sponsorship —** sets risk appetite, resolves major conflicts, and ensures governance does not become an isolated compliance exercise.

- **First line —** owns AI use cases, business outcomes, operational controls, and day-to-day system performance.

- **Second line —** sets policy, provides challenge, performs specialist review, and monitors aggregate AI risk.

- **Third line —** provides independent assurance without owning the controls it may later audit.

- **Affected stakeholders —** provide the perspective of people, customers, applicants, product users, and other groups whose interests may be materially affected.

## 6. Escalation Triggers

- Critical residual risk or proposed risk acceptance outside delegated authority.

- Potential prohibited or high-risk legal classification requiring specialist review.

- Material impact on employment, safety, fundamental rights, or vulnerable individuals.

- Significant AI incident, confidential-data leakage, discriminatory outcome, or loss of human oversight.

- Unregistered production AI use discovered outside the approved intake process.

- Material vendor/model change that invalidates prior evidence or assessment assumptions.
