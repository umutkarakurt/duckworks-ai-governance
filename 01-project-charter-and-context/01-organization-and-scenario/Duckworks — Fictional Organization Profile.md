# Duckworks — Fictional Organization Profile

**Duckworks** is a fictional technology and advanced manufacturing company specializing in the design and production of **mechanical and robotic arms engineered specifically for ducks**. Headquartered in the fictional European city of Pondhaven, Duckworks employs approximately 1,200 staff across engineering, manufacturing, software development, research, sales, customer support, cybersecurity, legal, HR, and corporate operations.

Its flagship products include lightweight mechanical assistive arms, precision robotic manipulators for industrial duck environments, and intelligent prosthetic systems capable of adapting to different species, body sizes, and occupational requirements.

Duckworks is currently undertaking an ambitious digital transformation program called **Project W.I.N.G. — Workflows, Intelligence, Next-Generation Governance**. As part of this initiative, the company intends to introduce artificial intelligence across a broad range of business processes.

## Planned AI Environment

Duckworks is evaluating or deploying several AI-enabled systems:

- **DuckDesign AI** — a generative engineering assistant used to propose mechanical-arm components, optimize designs, simulate load requirements, and assist engineers with CAD-related design decisions.
- **QuackBot** — a generative AI customer-service chatbot providing product information, troubleshooting guidance, warranty assistance, and technical support.
- **FeatherForecast** — a predictive analytics system used to forecast component demand, manufacturing requirements, supply-chain disruptions, and inventory levels.
- **WingInspect Vision** — a computer-vision system that analyzes manufacturing-line images to identify potential defects in mechanical arms and components.
- **DuckTalent AI** — an AI-supported employee screening and recruitment system that evaluates job applications, compares candidates against predefined role criteria, summarizes CVs, and recommends candidates for further review.
- **PondGPT** — an internal generative AI assistant used by employees for document drafting, research, knowledge retrieval, meeting summaries, coding support, and operational questions.

The organization expects additional AI projects to emerge as individual departments experiment with new technologies. Duckworks therefore wants to establish a formal AI governance capability before AI adoption becomes fragmented, poorly controlled, or difficult to inventory.

## Key Duckworks Characters

### Dr. Mallory Duckworth — Chief Executive Officer

Mallory Duckworth is the ambitious CEO behind Project W.I.N.G. She believes AI can transform Duckworks from a traditional manufacturer into the world's leading intelligent assistive-technology company for ducks.

She strongly supports rapid experimentation and frequently asks:

*"Why are we still doing this manually?"*

Her enthusiasm for innovation occasionally creates tension with governance teams concerned about introducing AI faster than the organization can adequately assess its risks.

### Professor Archibald McDuck — Chief Technology Officer

Archibald McDuck is an eccentric mechanical engineer and roboticist responsible for Duckworks' engineering, software, data, and AI strategy.

He is fascinated by generative design and wants DuckDesign AI integrated directly into engineering workflows.

Archibald values technical performance and experimentation but has historically viewed documentation, risk assessments, and approval processes as obstacles to innovation.

### Cassandra Duckley — Chief Information Security Officer

Cassandra Duckley leads cybersecurity, information security governance, incident response, and technology risk.

She supports Duckworks' AI transformation but is particularly concerned about prompt injection, sensitive-data leakage, insecure AI integrations, third-party AI dependencies, model supply-chain risk, and uncontrolled employee use of public generative AI platforms.

Her unofficial motto is:

*"If it connects to the pond, it belongs in the threat model."*

### Beatrice Van Duck — Chief People Officer

Beatrice Van Duck leads HR and is the executive sponsor of **DuckTalent AI**.

She believes automated screening could reduce recruitment workload and improve consistency across hiring processes. However, she is concerned about candidate fairness, explainability, inappropriate automated decision-making, accessibility, inaccurate recommendations, and whether historical recruitment data could introduce undesirable patterns into the system.

She insists that humans should remain meaningfully involved in consequential employment decisions.

### Reginald Duckman — Chief Risk & Compliance Officer

Reginald Duckman oversees enterprise risk, compliance, internal controls, privacy coordination, and the emerging AI governance program.

He has been tasked with creating Duckworks' first **AI Governance Framework**, including an AI system inventory, risk classification methodology, approval process, control library, accountability model, monitoring requirements, and governance reporting.

Reginald's main challenge is balancing Duckworth's appetite for innovation with Cassandra's security concerns, Beatrice's employee-related responsibilities, and Archibald's desire to move quickly.

### Penelope Duckins — Head of Internal Audit

Penelope Duckins provides independent assurance over Duckworks' governance, risk management, and internal controls.

She does not own the AI governance framework but plans to assess whether Duckworks can demonstrate that AI systems are properly inventoried, assessed, approved, monitored, and supported by appropriate evidence.

Her favorite question is deceptively simple:

*"Can you prove that?"*

## AI Governance Objective

Duckworks wants to enable responsible AI adoption without unnecessarily restricting innovation. The organization therefore intends to establish a scalable governance model covering the complete AI lifecycle—from initial experimentation and procurement through deployment, monitoring, significant change, incident management, and retirement.

The central challenge for Duckworks is not simply deciding **whether AI should be used**, but determining **where it can be used, under what conditions, by whom, with what controls, and with what evidence demonstrating that those controls actually operate.**
