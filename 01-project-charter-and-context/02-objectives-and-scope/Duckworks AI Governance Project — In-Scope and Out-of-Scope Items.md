# Duckworks AI Governance Project — In-Scope and Out-of-Scope Items

## 1. Scope Purpose

The purpose of this scope definition is to establish clear boundaries for the initial Duckworks AI Governance Project.

The project is intended to design and demonstrate an enterprise AI governance capability for the fictional Duckworks organization. It focuses on governance, risk, control, accountability, and assurance activities associated with the organization’s current and planned AI use cases.

The project does not attempt to implement every technical, legal, operational, or certification activity that could eventually be required in a production environment.

---

# 2. In-Scope Items

## 2.1 AI Governance Operating Model

The project includes the design of an enterprise-level AI governance structure covering:

- governance roles and responsibilities;
- executive accountability;
- AI Governance Committee responsibilities;
- first-, second-, and third-line responsibilities;
- escalation and decision-making authorities;
- risk acceptance responsibilities;
- ownership requirements for AI systems;
- governance reporting arrangements.

The operating model will define how AI governance integrates with Duckworks' existing organizational structure.

## 2.3 AI Intake and Registration Process

# 3. Out-of-Scope Items

## 3.1 Production AI Development

The project does not include:

- coding production AI models;
- building production machine-learning pipelines;
- training models;
- configuring production infrastructure;
- deploying AI systems;
- maintaining production AI services.

Technical examples may be created for portfolio demonstration purposes only.

---

## 3.2 Full Technical Security Testing

The project does not include comprehensive:

- penetration testing;
- AI red teaming;
- adversarial model testing;
- vulnerability scanning;
- source-code review;
- API security testing;
- infrastructure penetration testing.

Security testing requirements may be defined, but full execution remains outside the initial project.

---

## 3.3 Formal Bias or Fairness Validation

The project identifies fairness risks and defines testing expectations, particularly for DuckTalent AI.

However, it does not include a complete statistical bias-validation exercise using real applicant data.

No real:

- protected-class data;
- demographic data;
- employment decisions;
- applicant records

are used.

Any testing data used in the portfolio is synthetic.

---

## 3.4 Legal Advice or Formal Legal Opinions

The project does not provide legal advice.

Regulatory mapping represents a governance and risk-screening exercise based on fictional assumptions and publicly available sources.

Formal determinations regarding:

- EU AI Act classification;
- GDPR compliance;
- employment law;
- product safety;
- cybersecurity law;
- liability

would require qualified legal review in a real organization.

---

## 3.5 Certification

The project does not claim:

- ISO/IEC 42001 certification;
- ISO/IEC 27001 certification;
- regulatory conformity;
- third-party attestation;
- independent certification.

Framework mappings are illustrative governance mappings only.

---

## 3.6 Formal Conformity Assessment

The project does not perform any legally required conformity assessment that may apply to a real high-risk AI system or regulated product.

It may identify the need for such an assessment but does not substitute for one.

---

## 3.7 Production Privacy Impact Assessments

The project includes privacy considerations within AI impact and risk assessments.

However, it does not constitute a formal GDPR Data Protection Impact Assessment based on real processing operations.

Where a real DPIA may be required, it would need to be completed separately using verified information.

---

## 3.8 Real Personal or Confidential Data

The project will not use:

- real employee records;
- real candidate CVs;
- real customer data;
- real supplier information;
- confidential government information;
- real incident data;
- proprietary employer documentation.

All data used for portfolio purposes will be fictional, synthetic, anonymized, or publicly available.

---

## 3.9 Real Vendor Due Diligence

The project may demonstrate a third-party AI assessment process.

It will not represent an actual due-diligence assessment of a named real-world provider unless based solely on public information and clearly identified as such.

---

## 3.10 Enterprise Procurement Implementation

The project does not redesign Duckworks' complete procurement process.

Instead, it defines AI-specific governance requirements that should integrate with procurement and vendor-management activities.

---

## 3.11 Full Software Development Lifecycle Redesign

The project does not replace Duckworks' entire SDLC.

It will identify AI-specific governance checkpoints that should be incorporated into existing development and change-management processes.

---

## 3.12 Full Enterprise Risk Management Redesign

The project does not replace Duckworks' enterprise risk framework.

AI risk-management processes should integrate with the existing enterprise risk architecture.

---

## 3.13 Operational Implementation of Monitoring Platforms

The project may define required:

- KPIs;
- KRIs;
- dashboards;
- alerts;
- monitoring thresholds.

However, implementation of a production monitoring platform is outside scope.

---

## 3.14 Production Incident Response Operations

The project may define AI incident-response requirements and workflows.

It does not include operation of:

- a SOC;
- incident-response team;
- forensic investigation capability;
- live breach response.

---

## 3.15 Employee Training Delivery

The project may develop training requirements, awareness topics, or example training materials.

Delivery of an enterprise-wide training program to real employees is outside scope.

---

## 3.16 Board or Executive Approval

The project may simulate governance decisions and approval workflows.

It does not represent approval from a real:

- board of directors;
- executive committee;
- risk committee;
- regulator;
- legal function.

# 4. Scope Boundaries

The following boundaries apply throughout the project:

### Organizational Boundary

The project covers the fictional Duckworks organization and its initial AI portfolio.

### Geographic Boundary

Duckworks is assumed to operate primarily within the European market.

Additional jurisdictional requirements are outside scope unless specifically introduced into a future project phase.

### Data Boundary

Only fictional, synthetic, or publicly available data may be used.

### Technology Boundary

The project is technology-neutral.

It focuses on governance requirements rather than prescribing a specific:

- cloud provider;
- model;
- AI vendor;
- GRC platform;
- development framework.

### Assurance Boundary

The project demonstrates **readiness for assurance**, not an independent assurance conclusion.

# 6. Scope Principle

The Duckworks AI Governance Project follows the principle:

> **Govern what materially affects the organization, its stakeholders, or its obligations—without attempting to solve every AI-related problem within a single project.**

This boundary allows the project to remain realistic, auditable, and achievable while still demonstrating the core components of an enterprise AI governance program.
