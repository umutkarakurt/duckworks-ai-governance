# Duckworks AI Governance Project — Project Objectives

## 1. Primary Objective

The primary objective of the Duckworks AI Governance Project is to establish a **practical, risk-based, and auditable AI governance framework** that enables Duckworks to adopt artificial intelligence across the organization while maintaining appropriate oversight of legal, regulatory, security, privacy, fairness, safety, operational, and ethical risks.

The framework should support innovation without applying unnecessary governance burden to low-impact AI use cases.

---

## 2. Specific Project Objectives

### 2.1 Establish Enterprise-Wide AI Visibility

Create and maintain a centralized inventory of AI systems and AI-enabled use cases across Duckworks.

The inventory should identify, at minimum:

- system name and intended purpose;
- business owner;
- technical owner;
- lifecycle stage;
- AI technology type;
- affected individuals or groups;
- data categories processed;
- third-party providers;
- human-oversight arrangements;
- risk classification;
- assessment status;
- approval status;
- monitoring requirements.

The inventory should include both formally approved systems and discovered **shadow or unregistered AI usage**.

---

### 2.2 Define Clear AI Governance Accountability

Establish clear accountability for AI throughout the organization.

This includes defining responsibilities for:

- executive management;
- business owners;
- technical owners;
- AI developers and data teams;
- Information Security;
- Risk and Compliance;
- Privacy;
- Legal;
- Human Resources;
- Procurement;
- Internal Audit.

The project should establish an **AI Governance Committee** and define its decision-making, escalation, and reporting responsibilities.

---

### 2.3 Develop a Consistent AI Risk Classification Methodology

Create a repeatable methodology for determining the level of governance required for each AI system.

The methodology should consider factors including:

- potential impact on individuals;
- potential safety consequences;
- financial and operational impact;
- cybersecurity exposure;
- privacy impact;
- discrimination and fairness risks;
- level of automation;
- degree of human oversight;
- business criticality;
- third-party dependency;
- regulatory significance.

The methodology should distinguish between:

- internal enterprise risk ratings; and
- legal or regulatory classifications.

---

### 2.4 Assess AI Risks Across the Initial Portfolio

Apply the approved risk methodology to the initial seven Duckworks AI inventory entries.

For each system, the project should identify:

- relevant threat and risk scenarios;
- inherent risk;
- existing controls;
- control effectiveness;
- residual risk;
- target residual risk;
- risk-treatment requirements;
- approval or deployment conditions.

Risk conclusions should be traceable to documented assumptions and evidence.

---

### 2.5 Evaluate the Wider Impact of AI Systems

Perform structured AI impact assessments for material AI systems.

The impact assessments should consider potential effects on:

- employees;
- applicants;
- customers;
- business partners;
- suppliers;
- product users;
- organizational operations;
- safety;
- privacy;
- fairness;
- human autonomy;
- fundamental rights;
- information security.

The assessment process should consider both **positive and adverse impacts**.

---

### 2.6 Establish a Risk-Based AI Approval Process

Define a governance process for approving, conditionally approving, rejecting, suspending, or retiring AI systems.

Approval requirements should be proportional to risk.

For example:

- lower-impact systems may follow a simplified review;
- higher-risk systems may require enhanced assessment, testing, legal review, executive approval, and ongoing monitoring;
- unacceptable use cases should not proceed until material risks have been adequately addressed.

---

### 2.7 Develop an AI Control Framework

Design a structured library of AI governance and technical controls.

The control framework should address areas including:

- AI inventory management;
- risk assessment;
- impact assessment;
- data governance;
- privacy;
- security;
- human oversight;
- fairness;
- transparency;
- model validation;
- third-party risk;
- change management;
- logging;
- monitoring;
- incident management;
- system retirement.

Each material control should identify:

- control objective;
- control owner;
- frequency;
- expected evidence;
- testing approach;
- applicable AI risks.

---

### 2.8 Integrate AI Governance with Existing Enterprise Processes

Ensure that AI governance is not implemented as an isolated compliance activity.

The framework should integrate with existing Duckworks processes for:

- enterprise risk management;
- information security;
- privacy;
- procurement;
- vendor management;
- software development;
- product engineering;
- change management;
- incident management;
- business continuity;
- internal audit.

Where possible, existing governance processes should be extended rather than duplicated.

---

### 2.9 Strengthen Third-Party AI Governance

Establish minimum governance requirements for external AI providers, APIs, hosted models, and AI-enabled SaaS products.

The project should define requirements for assessing:

- vendor security;
- privacy practices;
- data retention;
- data usage;
- model training practices;
- subcontractors;
- service availability;
- intellectual-property considerations;
- incident notification;
- model or service changes;
- contractual protections;
- exit and transition arrangements.

---

### 2.10 Address Shadow and Unregistered AI Usage

Create a formal approach for discovering and managing AI tools used outside approved processes.

The project should establish:

- acceptable-use requirements;
- prohibited-use conditions;
- employee guidance;
- reporting channels;
- approved AI services;
- data-handling restrictions;
- discovery and monitoring mechanisms;
- remediation procedures.

The objective is not to prohibit reasonable AI experimentation but to ensure that experimentation occurs within defined organizational boundaries.

---

### 2.11 Establish AI Monitoring and Reassessment Requirements

Define the conditions under which AI systems must be continuously monitored or formally reassessed.

Monitoring should consider, where relevant:

- system performance;
- model drift;
- security events;
- harmful or incorrect outputs;
- fairness indicators;
- override rates;
- complaints;
- incidents;
- control failures;
- vendor changes;
- model-version changes.

Reassessment should be triggered by material changes in:

- intended purpose;
- model;
- training or reference data;
- users;
- affected populations;
- integrations;
- vendors;
- regulatory requirements;
- operating environment.

---

### 2.12 Establish Evidence and Auditability Requirements

Ensure that Duckworks can demonstrate how material AI governance decisions were made.

Required governance evidence may include:

- AI inventory records;
- risk assessments;
- impact assessments;
- approvals;
- testing results;
- validation records;
- security assessments;
- legal or privacy reviews;
- human-oversight procedures;
- monitoring records;
- incident records;
- committee decisions;
- exceptions and risk acceptances.

The objective is to move from:

> **“We believe this AI system is controlled.”**

to:

> **“We can demonstrate how it is controlled and provide evidence that those controls operate.”**

---

### 2.13 Support Regulatory and Standards Readiness

Create a governance model that can support assessment against relevant legislation, standards, and recognized frameworks.

Project reference sources include, where applicable:

- EU Artificial Intelligence Act;
- GDPR;
- relevant employment and equality legislation;
- product-safety and cybersecurity legislation;
- ISO/IEC 42001;
- ISO/IEC 23894;
- ISO/IEC 42005;
- ISO/IEC 27001;
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework;
- recognized AI cybersecurity guidance.

The project does not seek to claim certification or automatic legal compliance.

Instead, it aims to establish **traceable governance practices that can support future compliance and assurance activities**.

---

### 2.14 Enable Management-Level AI Risk Reporting

Develop management reporting that provides decision-makers with a clear view of the Duckworks AI portfolio.

Reporting should include:

- number of AI systems;
- systems by lifecycle stage;
- systems by risk level;
- systems awaiting approval;
- high and critical residual risks;
- overdue remediation actions;
- unassessed systems;
- third-party AI exposure;
- significant incidents;
- upcoming reassessments;
- control implementation status.

Management reporting should enable prioritization rather than simply presenting technical metrics.

---

### 2.15 Prepare Duckworks for Independent Assurance

Design the governance framework so that Internal Audit or another independent assurance function can later evaluate whether the controls are:

- appropriately designed;
- implemented;
- operating consistently;
- supported by adequate evidence.

Internal Audit should remain independent and should not own the governance controls it may later assess.

---

## 3. Target Project Outcome

At the completion of the initial project, Duckworks should be able to demonstrate that each material AI system has:

1. A documented intended purpose.
2. An accountable business owner.
3. An identified technical owner.
4. A valid AI inventory record.
5. A documented risk classification.
6. A completed risk assessment where required.
7. An impact assessment where required.
8. Identified regulatory and framework considerations.
9. Defined human-oversight arrangements.
10. Required controls and control owners.
11. A documented approval decision.
12. Monitoring and reassessment requirements.
13. Evidence supporting material governance decisions.
14. A defined process for escalation, suspension, and retirement.

---

## 4. Overall Success Principle

The Duckworks AI Governance Project will be considered successful when AI governance becomes an integrated business process rather than a one-time documentation exercise.

The intended operating principle is:

> **Every material AI use case should be known, owned, assessed, controlled, monitored, and evidenced in proportion to its potential impact.**
